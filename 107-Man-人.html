EN[I have wished to enter my earnest protest against the transmutation theory, revived of late with so much ability, and so generally received. It is my belief that naturalists are chasing a phantom in their search after some material gradation among created beings, by which the whole animal kingdom may have been derived by successive development from a single germ. There seems to me a repulsive poverty in this material explanation, that is contradicted by the intellectual grandeur of the universe: the resources of the Deity cannot be so meager that, in order to create a human being endowed with reason, He must change a monkey into a man. This theory is opposed to the processes of nature so far as we have been able to apprehend them; it is contradicted by the facts of embryology and paleontology—the former showing us forms of development as distinct and persistent for each group as are the fossil types of each period revealed to us by the latter. And the experiments upon domesticated animals and cultivated plants, on which its adherents base their views, are entirely foreign to the matter in hand, since the varieties thus brought about by the fostering care of man are of an entirely different character from those observed among wild species. Louis Agassiz.]

语法参：as … so … 关联结构（正如……照样……） → 008 as distinct and persistent for each group as are the fossil types：as … as 比较＋后半倒装。

中〔我一直想对『物种嬗变论』郑重提出抗议——这理论近来被人以极大的才力复兴，且被普遍接受。我相信：博物学家们在诸受造之物中间寻找某种物质性的渐变阶梯（以为整个动物界可由单一胚种逐次发育而来），不过是在追逐幻影。在我看来，这种物质性解释里有一种令人生厌的贫乏，与宇宙智性的宏伟正相抵触：神的资源不至于寒酸到这个地步——为要造出一个有理性的人，非得把一只猴子变成人不可。这理论与我们迄今所能领会的自然过程相悖；它也被胚胎学与古生物学的事实所否证——前者向我们展示：每一类群的发育形态各自分明而恒定，正如后者向我们揭示的每一时代的化石类型各自分明一样。至于该派论者据以立论的家养动物与栽培植物实验，与所论之事全然无关：因为在人的悉心培育之下产生的那些变种，与野生物种中所观察到的变异性质完全不同。路易·阿加西（Louis Agassiz，哈佛博物学家）〕

EN[As a compound being, made up of body and of spirit, man is the link which unites the natural and the spiritual worlds. The direct link between man and nature is the body, which—in its erect posture, its highly evolved brain, its developed limbs, and its countenance lifted up to the heavens—bears witness to the dignity of the soul within. As Materialism ignores the rights of the spirit, and would reduce thought, feeling, and will to functions of matter, so an ultra-spirituality is too apt to ignore the rights of the body, and to regard it as a mere accident of man's personality. The Bible avoids both extremes: Materialism gets all its rights in the Bible doctrine of the body. The Bible history of man's creation; the remarkable honor it places on the body as God's workmanship and the temple of the Holy Ghost; its doctrines of sin, with death as the penalty; of the Incarnation—"forasmuch as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, He also Himself likewise took part of the same"; of Redemption, which includes "the redemption of the body"; of the future life in a glorified corporeity—all warn us against an undue depreciation of the body. Prof. Orr.]

魂与灵；三分还是二分 / Soul and Spirit: Trichotomy or Dichotomy

EN2. Soul—Heb. nephesh; Gr. psuche (animal soul). 3. Spirit—Heb. ruach, wind; Gr. pneuma. Gen. 2:7. And breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul. Matt. 10:28; 16:26. Num. 16:22. The God of the spirits of all flesh. Heb. 12:9. The Father of spirits. 1 Thes. 5:23. Your whole spirit and soul and body. Prov. 20:27. The spirit of man is the candle of the Lord. [If the Bible gives its rightful place to the body, much more does it lay stress on the possession by man of a spirit, which is the true seat of his personality, and the link which unites him with the spiritual world and with God. In the Old Testament it is the unity of the personality which is the main fact, and not the distinction of an immaterial and a material part, as in our modern usage. Nephesh, or soul, does not in the Old Testament stand opposed to body, but is rather the principle of "life," which manifests itself on the one hand in the corporeal functions ("the life is in the blood"), and on the other in the conscious activities of the mind. The real contrast in the Old Testament is between "flesh" and "spirit," and the "soul" is the middle term between them, the unity of them. This does not mean that "soul" and "spirit" are separable elements in the same way that "soul" and "body" are; it means that the "soul," as inbreathed by God, is the source or seat of a double life. On the one side, it is the animating principle of the body, the source of all vital functions; it is its presence in the body which constitutes the latter "flesh." On the other side, it is the principle of self-conscious life; and the higher activities of the "soul"—those which we still denominate "spiritual"—are described by the term "spirit." Thus we read of a spirit of wisdom, of knowledge, of understanding; of an upright spirit, a free spirit, a contrite spirit.]

BSB1 Thessalonians 5:23 Now may the God of peace Himself sanctify you completely, and may your entire spirit, soul, and body be kept blameless at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.

语法参：同位语结构 → 001 nephesh / psuche / ruach / pneuma：四个原文词的同位注释——ruach 与 pneuma 本义皆为『风、气息』，故『灵』的意象源于呼吸。
语法the middle term between them：逻辑学术语『中项』（魂为肉与灵之间的中项）；inbreathed：被吹入的（创 2:7 的动词化）。

中2. 魂——希伯来文 nephesh；希腊文 psuche（有生命的魂／动物性的魂）。3. 灵——希伯来文 ruach（风）；希腊文 pneuma。创世记 2:7 —— 将生气吹在他鼻孔里，他就成了有灵的活人（英译：成了活的魂）。马太福音 10:28；16:26。民数记 16:22 —— 万人之灵的神。希伯来书 12:9 —— 万灵的父。帖撒罗尼迦前书 5:23 —— 又愿你们的灵与魂与身子得蒙保守。箴言 20:27 —— 人的灵是耶和华的灯。〔圣经若给了身体应得的地位，它就更加着重人拥有『灵』——灵是他位格真正的所在，是把他与属灵界、与神联结起来的那一环。在旧约里，主要的事实是位格的统一，而不是我们现代用法中『非物质部分』与『物质部分』的划分。旧约中的 nephesh（魂）并不与身体相对，倒是『生命』的原则：它一面显在身体的机能上（『活物的生命是在血中』），一面显在心智的自觉活动上。旧约里真正的对举是『肉体』与『灵』，而『魂』是介乎二者之间的中项、是二者的统一。这并不是说『魂』与『灵』像『魂』与『身体』那样是可分的元素；而是说：『魂』既由神吹入，就是一种双重生命的源头或所在。一面，它是身体的赋活原则、一切生命机能的源头；正因它在身体里，身体才成其为『肉体』。另一面，它是自觉生命的原则；而『魂』的较高活动——我们至今仍称之为『属灵的』那些——则以『灵』这个词来描述。所以我们读到智慧的灵、知识的灵、聪明的灵；正直的灵、乐意的灵、痛悔的灵。〕

EN[In the New Testament we have a distinction of "soul" and "body" much more akin to our own, though the influence of Old Testament usage is still very marked. "Soul" (psuche) still includes a higher and a lower life; and the higher life is still denoted by the term "spirit" (pneuma); while the implication of a body is still always conveyed in the term "soul." There is no "soul" which is not intended to animate a "body"; there are incorporeal spirits (angels, demons), but they are not called by the name "souls." On the other hand, the "soul" is recognized as spiritual in its essence, and in its disembodied state is classed among "spirits"—e.g., "the spirits in prison." Except in the sense above explained, I do not see how a trichotomous view of man's nature can be maintained. The distinction of "soul" and "spirit" is a distinction within the one indivisible spiritual nature; and the antithesis "soul" and "body" really covers all the facts of man's personal life. The highest functions of the "spirit" are in the New Testament ascribed also to the "soul"; and the "soul" in turn is used by Jesus as a name for man's highest imperishable life: "He that hateth his life (psuche) in this world shall keep it unto life eternal." Prof. Orr.]

语法trichotomous：三分的（灵魂体三分说）；akin to：与……相近；the antithesis "soul" and "body"：魂与体的对举（二分说 dichotomy）。
语法参：介词前置的关系从句 to whom / of which → 001 There is no "soul" which is not intended to animate a "body"：双重否定的关系从句（没有哪个魂不是为赋活身体而设的）。

中〔在新约里，『魂』与『身体』的区分与我们今日的用法要接近得多，尽管旧约用法的影响仍很显著。『魂』（psuche）仍旧兼含较高与较低的生命；较高的生命仍以『灵』（pneuma）一词标示；而『魂』这个词里也始终隐含着有身体。没有哪个『魂』不是为赋活一个『身体』而设的；有无形体的灵（天使、鬼魔），但它们不被称为『魂』。另一方面，『魂』的本质被认作属灵的；它在脱离身体的状态中被归入『灵』之列——例如『那些在监狱里的灵』。除上文所解释的意义之外，我看不出人性的『三分说』（trichotomy）如何能够成立。『魂』与『灵』的区分，是那唯一不可分之属灵本性内部的区分；而『魂』与『身体』的对举，才真正涵盖了人位格生命的全部事实。新约把『灵』的最高机能也归给『魂』；而耶稣反过来又用『魂』作为人最高、不朽之生命的名称：『在这世上恨恶自己生命（psuche）的，就要保守生命到永生。』奥尔教授〕

照神的形像造的 / Man Made in the Image of God

ENMan made in the image of God: Gen. 1:26, 27. And God said, Let Us make man in Our image, after Our likeness. So God created man in His own image. Gen. 5:1. Eph. 4:24. Put on the new man, which after God is created in righteousness and true holiness. Col. 3:10. Have put on the new man, which is renewed in knowledge after the image of Him that created him. [With the advent of man there appeared at the head of creation a moral and spiritual being—a being made in God's image, a rational and accountable being, a being for the first time capable of moral life, and bearing within him infinite possibilities of progress and happiness. At the summit of nature's gradations—of her inorganic kingdom and plant kingdom and animal kingdom—there stands a being fitted for the Kingdom of God. In a threefold respect is man the personal image of his Maker. 1. He bears the rational image of God: he can understand the world God has made; he is capable of reflection on himself, on the meaning and causes of things, on the ends of his own existence; he shows his superiority over nature through the thoughts he cherishes of God, of infinity, of eternity. 2. He bears God's moral image—not in the possession of actual righteousness, but in the possession of the indestructible elements of a moral nature. (1) He is a being with the power of moral knowledge: the idea of the good, and with it the moral "ought," is part of his constitution; throughout, man is a being who distinguishes good and evil, and who recognizes the obligation to obey the good and to eschew the evil. (2) He is a free, spiritual cause—that is, he has moral freedom. It is this power of will and self-decision which most of all constitutes him a person: through it he stands out of and above nature's sequences, and can react on and modify them. It is surely of little use to deny the possibility of miracle, when every human volition is a species of miracle—a new hyperphysical cause interpolated in the chain of physical events, and giving them a new direction. (3) Man is a being with moral affections; without this he would not be a true image of the God who is love. Summing up: we recognize in man a conscience which reveals moral law, a will which can execute moral purposes, and affections which create a capacity for moral love. 3. Man bears the image of God in his deputed sovereignty over the creatures. I might add one other mark, likewise involved in his self-conscious personality: what may be called the potential infinitude of his nature. It has often been remarked that man could not know himself to be finite if he were not able in thought to transcend the finite, and frame an idea of the Infinite. Though finite, hedged about on every side by the limitations of the finite, he yet shows a constant impulse to transcend these limitations, and ally himself with the Infinite. Through this peculiarity of his nature, there is none of God's infinite attributes which does not find a shadow in his soul. Prof. Orr.]

中人是照神的形像造的：创世记 1:26-27 —— 神说：我们要照着我们的形象、按着我们的样式造人……神就照着自己的形象造人。5:1。以弗所书 4:24 —— 并且穿上新人；这新人是照着神的形象造的，有真理的仁义和圣洁。歌罗西书 3:10 —— 穿上了新人；这新人在知识上渐渐更新，正如造他主的形象。〔随着人的出现，受造界之首出现了一位有道德、有灵性的存有——一个照神形像所造、有理性、须交账的存有，一个史上第一次能够过道德生活的存有，一个自身怀有无限进步与幸福之可能的存有。在自然的层级阶梯之巅——在她的无机界、植物界、动物界之上——站着一个适于进入神之国度的存有。人在三重意义上是造他者的位格形像。1. 他带着神理性的形像：他能理解神所造的世界；他能反思自己、反思万物的意义与成因、反思自身存在的目的；他借着自己心中所怀关于神、关于无限、关于永恒的思想，显出他高于自然。2. 他带着神道德的形像——不是在实际拥有义的意义上，而是在拥有道德本性中那些不可摧毁之元素的意义上。（1）他是一个具有道德认知能力的存有：『善』的观念，以及随之而来的道德之『应当』，是他构造的一部分；人自始至终是一个分辨善恶、并承认自己有义务顺从善、远避恶的存有。（2）他是一个自由的、属灵的原因——即他有道德的自由。正是这意志与自决之能，最主要地使他成为一个位格：借着它，他站在自然因果序列之外、之上，并能反作用于它、修改它。既然人的每一次意志活动都是某种神迹——一个新的『超物理原因』被插入物理事件之链中，赋予其新的方向——那么否认神迹的可能性，实在没有多大用处。（3）人是一个具有道德情感的存有；没有这一条，他就不是那位『就是爱』之神的真形像。总而言之：我们在人身上认出一个揭示道德律的良心、一个能执行道德目标的意志、以及创造出道德之爱之容量的诸般情感。3. 人在他受托统辖众受造之物的主权上，带着神的形像。我还可以加上一个标记，同样含在他自觉的位格里：可称之为其本性的『潜在无限性』。人们常说：人若不能在思想中超越有限、构想出『无限』的观念，就不可能知道自己是有限的。他虽有限、四面被有限的限制围住，却始终显出一种冲动，要超越这些限制、要把自己与无限联结起来。因着他本性中这一奇异之处，神无限的属性中没有哪一样，在人的灵魂里找不到一个影子。奥尔教授〕

心灵的官能与良心 / Faculties of the Spiritual Nature; Conscience

ENElemental endowments, or faculties, of man's spiritual nature: 1. Intellect, reason, understanding—including the powers of observation, perception, apprehension, conception, reflection, comparison, and judgment. 2. Heart (in the restricted sense), or sensibility—including the affections, emotions, and desires. 3. Will, or self-determining power—including preference and choice, purpose and decision. Of these three, only the intellect may be exercised alone, and then only upon abstract themes of thought. In the customary processes of mental action, the relation between the three may be stated in a single sentence: the wide-ranging intellect discloses to, and impresses upon, the sensibilities various objects of interest and attraction, and so excites emotion, affection, or desire; while, in their turn, stirred feeling and desire act with motive force upon the will, to choose and seek the desired objects.

EN4. Conscience, the moral faculty of man's nature. As a responsible, personally accountable being, man finds deeply implanted in the heart of his consciousness a threefold capacity and conviction concerning right and wrong, good and evil. He possesses, and knows that he possesses, a power of moral knowledge—an intuitive knowledge of the right and the wrong. He cherishes, and knows that he cherishes, in his inmost heart a power of moral affection—a capacity and intuitive demand for desiring and loving the right or the wrong. And he is profoundly conscious of the possession of a power of moral freedom in his every choice and act—a capacity of will that knowingly determines to seek, and determinedly seeks and enacts, the right or the wrong. This power, with its threefold media of acting, the Scripture calls Conscience; we name it, according to the faculty through which it acts, the moral judgment, the moral feeling, or the moral force. This Conscience, in its normal condition and attitude, dictates nothing other than the seeking of and obedience to the right and the good; and the actual response to its mandate determines the moral character of every human soul—that which he is in the sight of God, righteous or wicked, justified or condemned. Man is further endowed with an immortal and indestructible existence: this is a part of the original gift of God in the creation of man, entirely distinct from the faculties included in his spiritual nature; it is an added endowment—a bestowal of eternal existence for the enjoyment of being in the exercise and expansion of these powers. 伊甸的试验与堕落 / The Test in Eden and the Fall

ENAfter creation, man was blessed with home and work in Eden. Under the conscious smile, the fostering care, and the manifest love of his Divine Creator, he enjoyed the high, sweet privilege of fellowship and communion with his Father God—as intimated in Gen. 2:18-31, in the help meet made for and given to Adam, in the large and varied blessing of God upon them, and in the declaration immediately following: "And God saw everything that He had made, and, behold, it was very good." It is intimated also in Gen. 3:8-11, in the colloquy of God with Adam "in the garden in the cool of the day." In Eden man was placed under law by his Creator and Moral Governor. He was subjected to a simple test of love and obedience—a test of willingness to respond to his Creator's love, to conform his will to the sovereign will and rightful authority of the Infinite and Eternal God. A creature of intelligence, affection, and freedom in choice and action, knowing himself the recipient of his Creator's affection and bounty, he knew and felt a requital of affection and a response of obedience to be his natural and imperative obligation. And this love and obedience were the only elements of the law under which he was placed: from the nature and relations of God and His spiritual creatures there can be no other requirements than these. The particular test, too, was not a hard one: it demanded no positive or painful exertion; it asked only a small self-denial, the abstinence from a single self-indulgence which was itself absolutely needless for his comfort—nay, which his Creator's truthful word declared would prove destructive to his peace and happiness.

ENMan's failure under the test; his disobedience through self-indulgence; his rejection of Divine authority through self-will. It is true, and should be considered on the side of man—not as palliative or excuse of his transgression, but in simple justice to the first sinner as compared with multitudes among his successors—that his first movement toward disobedience did not come immediately from his own reasonings and determination: another agent, in the person of the head of already fallen angels, intervened to deceive and to tempt the untried man by false statements and insidious appeals. Thus, through the strength of excited desires in man's whole nature, and the weakness of his whole nature, a newly developed force of self-pride, self-indulgence, and self-will led the first pair deliberately to disobey God, to reject His love and fellowship, to set at nought and defy His sovereign, wise, and rightful authority. The resulting transformation of character and condition—a reversal of standing before God and His law of love and holiness: from his original innocence, righteousness, and purity; from favor with God and blessedness in God's companionship, he passed at once into a state of guilt and unrighteousness, of disfavor with God and banishment from His fellowship, and so of inward wretchedness. From thenceforth, too, he stood hopelessly, helplessly condemned before the Law and the Divine Lawgiver. From thenceforth all his high faculties were marred in their force and deranged in their acting: the reason became so darkened as to be almost impervious to the true knowledge of God; the heart became hardened against all appeal of high, pure desire and affection; the will became as adamant in resistance to all motive and urgency on the part of right and duty and love. And so all his spiritual powers have become misdirected and misused—turned backward from their originally adapted uses, away from God and toward self, independent and neglectful of God, dependent only upon and devoted to self.

ENMan's changed character and relations with God became the inheritance of his posterity. Simply the fact is to be noted; for the Scripture goes no farther, and reason has no basis for inference with respect to "the nature of the transmitted influence which connects our sin with the sin of Adam." But the truth that all are sinners finds frequent and varied expression in every book of the Bible. See Sin. The promise of mercy and deliverance through a personal Deliverer is embodied in the Divine words of sentence against Satan, against Eve, and against Adam (Gen. 3:14-19). The promise is contained in the sentence upon Satan: I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel. These words have always been emphasized as the Protevangelion, or First Gospel—the original root-promise of Jehovah. Its repetition and expansion, its increasing definiteness as to the Person who shall deliver and as to the details of His deliverance, and its final fulfillment in the Incarnation and atoning death of Christ, comprise the vital substance and breathing heart of the Old and New Testament revelation. A brief of points: Gen. 9:27—the Person limited to the race of Shem; later, to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob as progenitors of the nation Israel; again, to the tribe of Judah and the family of David is the final limitation. The method of deliverance was typically but effectually intimated in sacrifice—by the patriarchs unto Moses, and by the sacrificial system from Moses to Christ. Herein, as the Epistle to the Hebrews shows, Christ is disclosed as a Priest, Himself offerer and victim; by Moses He is revealed as a Prophet that should come; by David, as King. At length, in the exact "fullness of time," the Deliverer appeared in the Person of Jesus Christ, the Virgin-born Son of God, God manifest in flesh, and accomplished by His blameless, beneficent life—and pre-eminently by His self-sacrificial death—the great and long-promised deliverance.

人生的意义与两种生命 / Suggestive Truths; Two Types of Life

ENMan's dignity, greatness, and worth are to be measured: 1. By the mere fact of his Divine creation. 2. And by and through all these alternations, man's earthly life becomes a sphere and period of education, discipline, and training, for the perfecting of character here and hereafter. Man's chief aim and supreme duty in the earthly life consists in the supreme consecration of intellect, heart and will to God: this is the sum and substance of the great Law of Love.

ENTwo types of life comprise all living men. 1. The true God-centered life. In this life, the knowledge of God and His grace, the revealed truths and promises of His Word, and all questions of daily personal duty, are the predominant themes of thought and of prayerful study. In this life God Himself—as the source of all being, purity and blessedness—is the central and supreme object of the heart's deepest affection and longing, so that its impassioned cry is, "Whom have I in heaven but Thee? and there is none upon earth that I desire beside Thee." And in this life the will of God, as expressed in His law of holiness and love, is the single, all-inclusive, overruling principle of choice and of action, so that the subjected, believing, obedient man, looking ever in the face of God, can honestly say, "Thy will, not mine, be done." 2. The false self-centered life—the exact contrast to the true, at every point. Self is the only reality; self alone is the theme of chief, ever-present consideration, the supreme and exclusive object of affection and desire, the ultimate sole end of his daily aims, plans, and intense pursuit. God is far from his inmost controlling thoughts, and his life is ordered without reference to the will of God. The effects of that life upon the man himself: increasing detachment from and aversion to the God who is his true and eternal life; increasing grasp of the spirit of selfishness, narrowing his mental vision of vital truths, dwarfing and crushing out all high and holy affections, and all spiritual cravings for satisfying and abiding good; and utter collapse of all moral capacity for return to God. Upon the community, his influence runs in the direction of his own sad experience, under the force of his ever-active example; so that the self-centered man involves in his own downward progress all with whom his selfish interest has led him to associate. Nor can he do otherwise than sow thus widely the seeds of baleful influence, since no life is isolated—no man liveth to himself alone, though he live in utter selfishness.

中一切活着的人，都尽在两种生命类型之内。1. 真实的『以神为中心』的生命。在这生命里，对神与他恩典的认识、他话语中所启示的真理与应许、以及日常个人本分的一切问题，是思想与祷告式研读的主导题目。在这生命里，神自己——作为一切存有、纯洁与福乐的源头——是心中最深爱慕与切慕的中心与至上对象，以致这颗心热切的呼喊是：『除你以外，在天上我有谁呢？除你以外，在地上我也没有所爱慕的。』并且在这生命里，神那表达于圣洁与慈爱之律法中的旨意，是抉择与行动唯一的、涵盖一切的、居于统辖地位的原则；以致那降服、信靠、顺从的人，常常仰面注视神，能诚实地说：『愿你的旨意成就，不要成就我的意思。』2. 虚假的『以自我为中心』的生命——在每一点上都与真实的那种恰成对照。自我是唯一的实在；唯有自我是首要而时刻在场的考量题目，是情感与欲望至上而排他的对象，是他日常目标、计划与热切追逐的终极唯一目的。神远离他内心作主的思想，他的生活安排全不参照神的旨意。这种生活对他本人的效果是：与那位本是他真实永恒生命的神日益疏离、日益反感；自私之灵日益抓牢他，使他对攸关生命之真理的心智视野日益狭窄，把一切崇高圣洁的情感、一切对餍足恒久之美善的属灵渴求，都压矮压死；直至归回神的一切道德能力彻底崩塌。对社群而言，他的影响循着自己那可悲经历的方向而去——凭其时刻起作用的榜样之力；以致这以自我为中心的人，把一切因自私利益而与之结交的人，都卷进自己向下的滑落里。他也不可能不这样广泛地播撒有毒影响的种子；因为没有一个生命是孤立的——没有人是单为自己活的，纵然他活在彻底的自私之中。

中人真正的荣耀：认识神、爱神、肖似神、事奉神。耶利米书 9:23-24 —— 耶和华如此说：智慧人不要因他的智慧夸口，勇士不要因他的勇力夸口，财主不要因他的财物夸口。夸口的却因他有聪明，认识我是耶和华，又知道我喜悦在世上施行慈爱、公平和公义，以此夸口。哥林多前书 1:31 —— 夸口的当指着主夸口。罗马书 5:3 —— 就是在患难中也是欢欢喜喜的（英译：也以患难夸口）。加拉太书 6:14 —— 但我断不以别的夸口，只夸我们主耶稣基督的十字架。彼得前书 5:1, 10 —— 同享后来所要显现之荣耀的人。以上各端都引向那至高的一点：人不变而不朽的生命。这生命在于他一切属灵官能无尽的延续与扩展，其结果是知识、情感与服事的不断拓宽。今生本质的价值，就在于它与来世的关系、在于它为来世所作的预备：地上所拥有、所经历、所做成的一切，唯有在其有助于得着永生这一点上才有价值。有一句话就道尽了这最终而攸关生命之要点的全部实质：那位道成肉身、代赎、复活、得荣的基督，就是人得以完全、得以长存之生命的源头与赐予者。
